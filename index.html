A Reading List in the Key of Loss

Blue Hours

Artifacts of Grief

Seven works — memoirs, a novel-essay, a film, a musical — that sit with the unbearable and try to make a shape for it. Each kept its own shade of blue.

Memoir · 2005

The Year of Magical Thinking

Joan Didion

Didion's husband died of a heart attack at the dinner table while their daughter lay in a coma. This is the chronicle of the year that followed — and of the irrational certainty, the magical thinking, that if she kept his shoes he would need them to come back.

Grief here is not a feeling but a derangement of logic: the mind bargaining against a fact it cannot hold.

Image description

Cream cover; "JOAN DIDION" in tall spaced black serif caps (one letter blue); title in three stacked lines; gold National Book Award seal, top right.

Memoir · 2011

Blue Nights

Joan Didion

A companion to the loss of her husband: now Didion writes through the death of her daughter, Quintana. The title names the long luminous twilights near the solstice — the blue hours when the light seems endless, just before it fails.

It is a book about how brightness fades: of children, of memory, of one's own body. Mourning braided with the fear of one's own ending.

Image description

Near-white, icy-blue cover; "JOAN DIDION" large in blue serif caps up top; small "author of The Year of Magical Thinking"; "BLUE NIGHTS" in navy caps below.

Lyric Essay · 2009

Bluets

Maggie Nelson

Two hundred and forty numbered fragments addressed to the color blue — and underneath the devotion, two woundings: the end of a love affair, and a dear friend left paralyzed by a fall.

Nelson asks what it means to fall in love with a color, and answers something harder: how we attach ourselves to what cannot be kept, and call that attachment a life.

Image description

Midnight-blue cover with a cloudy, star-flecked watery texture; a blank white square floats near center; small text below reads "MAGGIE NELSON" (left) and "BLUETS" (right).

Memoir · 2017

Dear Friend, from My Life I Write to You in Your Life

Yiyun Li

Written across two years of hospitalization for suicidal depression, Li turns to the writers she loves — Mansfield, Stefan Zweig — as a way of staying in the world. It is a grief turned inward: the mourning of a self that does not want to remain.

Language, here, is not consolation but tether — the thin thread by which one is held to living.

Image description

Teal-and-green watercolor wash, darker at the foot; "YIYUN LI" very large in dark serif; the title woven through in white italic script.

Memoir · 2025

Bibliophobia

Sarah Chihaya

A memoir of the books that made Chihaya — and of the breakdown that reading could neither prevent nor explain. She traces a lifelong belief that the right book might finally name her, and the collapse that followed when none did.

Grief for an imagined self: the one the stories promised, who never arrived.

Image description

Flat green cover; "BIBLIOPHOBIA" repeated in pale cream serif at the top, lines tilting as if toppling; "SARAH CHIHAYA" in pink near the foot; "A MEMOIR" printed upside-down at the bottom edge.

Film · dir. Lukas Dhont · 2022

Close

Lukas Dhont

Two thirteen-year-old boys share a friendship of rare tenderness — until the gaze of others makes them flinch from it, and one pulls away. What follows is a sudden, irreversible loss, and the survivor's wordless grief and guilt.

A study of mourning at an age with no language for it: the ache of having drawn back a moment too far.

Image description

Two fair-haired boys (~13) embrace; one rests against the other's shoulder, green eyes to camera over a red sweater. "CLOSE" in thin white spaced caps in the lower third; star-rating quotes and a Cannes 2022 laurel.

Musical · 2016

Dear Evan Hansen

Pasek & Paul / Steven Levenson

After a classmate's suicide, an anxious, invisible teenager is mistaken for the dead boy's only friend — and lets the lie grow, because for the first time a grieving family lets him belong.

A story about grief as a thing the living use: how mourning can be borrowed, performed, and ache all the same. The cast on his arm, a signature no one came to write.

Image description

Cropped close on a teenager's torso in a blue horizontally striped polo against pale blue; one forearm in a white plaster cast; bold white block letters read "DEAR EVAN HANSEN."

Coda

Hold the Blue

Grief keeps no single form. It arrives as a deranged logic, a fading light, a color one cannot stop loving, a friendship withdrawn, a self mislaid, a lie that comforts. These artifacts do not resolve it. They keep it company.
